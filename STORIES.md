## Heroes (drawn, 1–3)

1. **Riyadh, Saudi Arabia — finance-district tower targeted.**
   The Saudi-led coalition said Tuesday that a skyscraper in
   Riyadh's financial district had been targeted by a "terrorist
   attack" and that competent authorities were handling it; no
   further details were given. Context from the same report: the
   Houthis have been firing at Saudi airports and oil
   infrastructure since Yemen's frozen civil war reignited in
   July; Riyadh's tallest skyscraper (the sovereign wealth fund's
   HQ) was struck earlier this month, and the Aramco building was
   hit on 8 November. Labelled OFFICIAL: the coalition's own
   statement. Source: Reuters (Tue 24 Nov, filed <1h before the
   15:23 UTC crawl; article read in full).
2. **Beijing, China — Politburo member investigated.** Shanghai
   party chief Chen Jining, a member of the Politburo, is under
   investigation, Xinhua said Tuesday — the fourth member of the
   top leadership body to be probed since 2025. Labelled
   OFFICIAL. Source: Reuters (Tue 24 Nov).
3. **Washington, USA — backlash over Huang's return.** Trump
   faces backlash from politicians on both sides after saying he
   'convinced' Nvidia to reinstate CEO Jensen Huang (BBC);
   Germany's Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul said Tuesday he
   was "shocked" by Trump's intervention to pressure Nvidia
   (Reuters). Trump owns millions of dollars in Nvidia shares
   (BBC). Sources: BBC, Reuters (Tue 24 Nov).

## Grid panels (drawn, 4–11)

4. **Zaporizhzhia, Ukraine — overnight attack.** A Russian
   attack killed 2 people and injured 22, including 2 children,
   in Zaporizhzhia overnight, regional defence council co-head
   Ivan Fedorov said on Telegram on Tuesday. Labelled CLAIM:
   Reuters could not independently confirm the report. Source:
   Reuters (Tue 24 Nov).
5. **Hanoi, Vietnam — explosion kills six.** At least 6 people
   were killed and 10 injured in an explosion at 08:00 local
   (01:00 UTC) at a food shop on Dong Da Street, in a densely
   populated residential area; nearby windows shattered. Police
   and state media via Reuters (filed Tue 07:52 UTC).
6. **Caracas, Venezuela — debt at $237.5bn.** Venezuela's
   government published its long-awaited macroeconomic framework
   on Tuesday, putting its debts at $237.5 billion and projecting
   the economy to more than double in five years to $200.9
   billion — a step that could underpin one of the world's
   largest-ever debt restructurings. Source: Reuters (Tue).
7. **Paris, France — Nahel officer to face murder trial.** A
   French police officer will stand trial for murder in the
   killing of Nahel Merzouk, 17, in Nanterre on 27 June 2023,
   which sparked violent protests across the country; the
   Versailles appeals court ruled Tuesday there was sufficient
   evidence. Source: Reuters (Tue 10:29 UTC).
8. **Honiara, Solomon Islands — pact swap.** The Solomon
   Islands has informed China it will terminate the 2022
   security agreement and not renew it when it expires in April;
   a new comprehensive treaty with Australia is expected to be
   signed when PM Matthew Wale meets PM Anthony Albanese on
   Wednesday. Labelled REPORTED: the Sydney Morning Herald,
   reported by Reuters (Tue).
9. **London, UK — Houthi sanctions.** Britain on Tuesday
   designated the Houthis as an entity under its Yemen sanctions
   regime and announced measures against four senior figures,
   accusing them of involvement in torture, rape and other
   serious abuses; the UK said the move followed the Houthis
   resuming conflict, including targeting Saudi civilian
   infrastructure. Source: Reuters (Tue 12:03 UTC).
10. **Baghdad, Iraq — Kurdistan crossings takeover.** PM Ali
    al-Zaidi ordered the border crossings authority to take over
    all crossings in the Kurdistan region and the federal oil
    ministry to manage fields under Kurdish security control in
    four provinces, after an oil smuggling probe, Iraq's state
    news agency said Tuesday. Labelled OFFICIAL. Source:
    Reuters (Tue).
11. **Brussels — Nato space vow.** The Nato chief vowed defence
    against a space threat after reports of a Russian nuclear
    satellite programme. Source: BBC (Tue 24 Nov). Note: a
    reference page dated this to Monday 23 November; no Monday
    reporting supported it — it verified as a Tuesday story, so
    it runs today (see DATE CHECK).
